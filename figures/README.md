# IO-HMM recovery & identifiability figures

Generated by [`scripts/make_recovery_figures.py`](../scripts/make_recovery_figures.py).
The PNGs are git-ignored (regenerable); this README records what they show and
the quantified thresholds behind the test-suite design.

```bash
python scripts/make_recovery_figures.py            # full (≈ few min)
python scripts/make_recovery_figures.py --quick    # fast, fewer seeds
```

The IO-HMM faces **two distinct identifiability constraints**. Conflating them
is what made early recovery scenarios look like they needed "extreme" states;
in fact only the second constraint forces wide state separation.

## `fig1_psych_recovery.png` — psychometric recovery *in isolation*

Single-state (`K=1`, latent label known) fits, many seeds, long sessions.
This is "what is recoverable in principle" once state attribution is removed.

| Parameter | Recoverable range | Per-seed sd | Notes |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------|-------|
| `alpha` (bias)   | across ±2        | ≤ 0.08 | dead on identity |
| `gamma` (lapse)  | across 0–0.3     | ≈ 0.01 | needs boundary-spanning conditions |
| `beta` (slope)   | **only up to ~1.5** | grows fast beyond | saturation wall (below) |

**Why `beta` saturates.** The IO log-posterior-odds `g` already span ≈ ±5 over
the condition grid, so `P(go) = σ(α + β·g)` is pinned near 0/1 for `β ≳ 1.5`:
`β = 2` vs `β = 3` produce near-identical choices (0.970 vs 0.975 at the
boundary). The γ-weighted slope MLE then undergoes logistic **separation** and
runs to the bound (true `β = 5` → recovered ≈ 12). This is the concrete,
quantified motivation for **v0.5 velocity emissions**: richer per-trial signal
would break the saturation that choices alone cannot.

Panel (d) shows ordinary shallow / biased / lapsing curves (true vs recovered
overlapping) — the identifiable regime is realistic, not degenerate.

## `fig2_hmm_separability.png` — joint HMM recovery is gated by separation

Choice-only emissions carry **~1 bit/trial**, so the latent *path* (and hence
per-state parameter attribution) is recoverable only when the states are
behaviourally separated.

- **(a)** Permutation-corrected Viterbi agreement vs the bias gap
  `|α_Hi − α_Lo|` between two states (β fixed). Agreement sits at chance (~50%)
  for gaps ≤ 2 and climbs through **73% → 79% → 88%** at gaps 3 → 4 → 6.
  Threshold ≈ **bias gap 3**.
- **(b)** A well-separated 3-state fit clears that bar: the transition matrix
  recovers to `|A_err| ≈ 0.013` with ~86% path agreement.

**Takeaway.** The recovery tests use widely-spaced states for *separability*
(constraint 2), not because milder psychometric values are unrecoverable
(constraint 1 shows they are). v0.5 velocity emissions help on *both* axes, but
the bits/trial increase in (a) is arguably the bigger structural win.

> Note on exchangeable states: when two states share the same free-param
> signature they are label-exchangeable, so EM may converge to a permuted
> solution. The separability panel uses permutation-corrected agreement;
> the test suite sidesteps this by giving each state a distinct fixed-param
> signature.

## `fig3_velocity_emissions.png` — adding trial-by-trial channels

Generated by [`scripts/velocity_emission_demo.py`](../scripts/velocity_emission_demo.py).
Standalone proof-of-concept (reuses `io_core` + the HMM core, no production
dependency) for whether a second channel — pre-reward-zone **velocity** — breaks
the two walls above.

- **(a) slope wall.** A binary choice is a 1-bit quantisation of `d = α + β·g`,
  which is why β saturates. A *graded* readout of the same `d` recovers β to 5
  with sd ≈ 0.02 — **but only if its decision-coupling gain is calibrated.**
  With a free gain the velocity mean is `κ·β·g`, so only the product `κβ` is
  identified: a free-gain readout is degenerate with β and does *not* rescue the
  slope (it is actually worse). So velocity buys the slope only under a
  gain-calibration assumption.
- **(b) separability wall.** A per-state velocity *marker* (engagement: means
  separated by `d′`, stimulus-independent) decodes states the choices cannot:
  at `d′ = 2`, Viterbi agreement is **95% even at bias gap 0.5**, where
  choice-only is ~59%. This holds for *any* state-distinguishing channel and is
  the robust, unconditional win.

**Status: superseded by the full confidence model.** The production velocity
channel is no longer a bare marker. `fit(..., use_velocity=True)` now fits the
paper's confidence-velocity emission `v ~ N(beta_vel·DV(m) + alpha_vel,
sigma_vel)`, marginalised over the latent `m` *jointly with choice* (see
`emissions.joint_log_emission_state`, `fit._fit_state_joint`,
`io_core.decision_variable`). The marker characterised here is exactly its
`beta_vel = 0` special case. Crucially this resolves the (a) gain-calibration
worry: `DV(m)` is computed from the **frozen** Stage-1 `kappa`, so `beta_vel`
(the confidence gain) is identified — no free sensory gain to be degenerate
with. `tests/test_io_hmm_velocity.py` recovers `beta_vel` to ~2.49 (true 2.5)
with engaged/disengaged separation, and the `beta_vel = 0` marker case still
recovers baselines at >0.9.

## `fig4_velocity_intuition.png` / `fig5_velocity_doseresponse.png` — intuition

Generated by [`scripts/velocity_intuition_figures.py`](../scripts/velocity_intuition_figures.py),
which runs the *production* `fit(..., use_velocity=True)` in its `beta_vel = 0`
(stimulus-independent baseline) regime. Two states with a small choice bias gap
(1.0) but distinct velocity baselines.

- **fig4 (a)** psychometric curves nearly overlap → choices barely separate the
  states; **(b)** their velocity distributions are well separated, and the fitted
  markers (dashed) land on the modes; **(c)** trial-by-trial Viterbi: choice-only
  flips near chance (~56%) while choice+velocity tracks the true path (~97%).
- **fig5** dose-response: choice-only stays flat at ~56% for all d′ (the channel
  is uninformative for these states), while choice+velocity climbs **59% → 98%**
  as the marker d′ grows (d′≈1 already ~82%).

The one-line intuition: *the latent state lives in whatever channel separates
it.* Choices barely encode the Lo/Hi distinction, so a choice-only HMM is near
chance; velocity encodes it, so adding that channel makes the states
recoverable.

## `fig6_perception_dissociation.png` — free perception (Phase 2)

Generated by [`scripts/perception_dissociation_figure.py`](../scripts/perception_dissociation_figure.py).
Phase 2 opens each state's **perceptual front-end** to EM: a sensory-precision
multiplier `lambda_` and a parameterised task prior (`prior_strength`,
`prior_weight`). The headline is a **perception–action dissociation** — states
with the *same* choice/action parameters but different perception are separated
and the perceptual parameter is recovered, *because* the velocity channel reads
`DV(m)` independently of the choice psychometric. The recovery setups mirror
`tests/test_io_hmm_perception.py`. See `ideal_observer/io_hmm/REPORT.md` for the
full write-up.

- **(a) mechanism.** `lambda_` multiplies the Stage-1 `kappa` for *both* the
  generative `p(m|s)` and the inference posterior, so it reshapes the expected
  decision variable `E[DV(m)|s]` — sharper (`lambda_>1`) or blurrier (`lambda_<1`).
  The choice psychometric, by contrast, only rescales an otherwise-fixed `DV`.
- **(b) `lambda_` ⊥ action.** Sharp (`lambda_=2`) vs Blur (`lambda_=0.5`) with
  identical `beta = beta_vel = 2`. Free `lambda_` recovers **Sharp 1.64 / Blur
  0.53 (ratio 3.1×)** while the action gain `beta_vel` stays at **≈1.99 / 1.89**
  for both — perception moves, action does not. Viterbi agreement **97%**. (The
  recovered Sharp value sits below true 2.0: near `lambda_~2` the posterior is
  already near-saturated — the analogue of the `beta` slope wall — so the ratio
  and ordering recover more tightly than the absolute high-precision value.)
- **(c) perceptual category bias.** A free `prior_weight` (mass on the 0-deg Go
  centre) recovers **Lo 0.27 / Hi 0.69** (true 0.25 / 0.75) for two states whose
  choice/action match; agreement **98%**. This is a *perceptual* bias upstream of
  the decision, distinct from the decisional bias `alpha`.

**Fragility note.** Fitting `lambda_` *and* `prior_strength` free per state is
degenerate (both sharpen the posterior); the default `with_perception` wiring
frees only `lambda_`, and the suite covers the both-free case for monotonicity /
plumbing only, not joint recovery.
