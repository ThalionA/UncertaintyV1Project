## 2026-07-29 — flat-evar (`shape_lambda → ∞`) × zero hidden units × neural-PC ladder (registered BEFORE the `flatevar_v1` launch)

**P1 — flat_evar at H=8 lands peakiness on target and beats chance.** `flat_evar` is the λ→∞ limit of `shape_lambda` (uniform per-PC weights = unweighted Brier), and `shape_lambda=30` already sits at ~96% Brier with peakiness 0.057/0.058 and normalised loss 0.78/0.75. So flat_evar should land within noise of that, i.e. peakiness ≈0.05–0.07 and normalised loss ≈0.72–0.85 (spat/temp). Confidence ~85%. Falsifier: peakiness >1.5× target, or normalised loss ≥1 (a lobotomy). *A meaningfully BETTER result than shape30 would also be informative* — it would say the residual evar weighting at λ=0.3 is still doing harm.

**P2 — THE DECISIVE ONE: flat_evar + zero hidden units also lands on target.** `prodfix_v1` arm C showed the projection loss over-sharpens 5.6×/10.5× with no hidden layer at all, which killed the capacity account and left loss geometry as the explanation. If the evar weighting is genuinely the cause, then removing the weighting must fix it *even with no capacity to regularise*. Predict `B_flat_linear` peakiness ≈ the IO target (≤1.3×) in ≥5/6 mice. Confidence ~80%. **Falsifier: if `B_flat_linear` still over-sharpens ≥2× target, the loss-geometry account is wrong or incomplete** — the pathology would then have to come from something shared by both weightings (the softmax parameterisation, or the location/shape split itself) rather than from the evar weighting. This is the single most informative cell in the run.

**P3 — the other hyperparameters stop mattering once the weighting is flat.** Under evar weighting, only early stopping moved peakiness (temporal 0.72→0.27, still ~4.5× target), and dropout only at p=0.9 (a lobotomy). Predict that under flat weighting the OAT sweep (width 2–32, dropout, wd, λ_H, early stop) leaves peakiness within ~±30% of the flat baseline across every axis except `weight_decay=1e-2`, which will still collapse to the uniform decoder (peakiness →0.011, normalised loss ~1.6) because that is an optimisation-killing dose, not a calibration effect. Confidence ~70%. Falsifier: any generic knob moving peakiness by >2× under flat weighting — that would mean the weighting was masking a capacity effect all along.

**P4 — the neural-PC ladder saturates early and never beats the full population.** The smoke run already showed 4 PCs retain only **18.7%** of z-scored input variance in mouse 0 (65 neurons), so low-k cells are heavily lossy. Predict normalised loss improves monotonically with k and is statistically indistinguishable from the full-population cell by k≈32, with no k beating it. Confidence ~60% — this is the weakest prior of the four, because if V1 uncertainty coding lives in a low-dimensional subspace a mid-k cell could genuinely win. **Bonus prior (~65%): `C_evar_npc16` — input-side PCA with the evar weighting still on — will NOT fix over-sharpening** (peakiness stays ≥3× target), because input-side dimensionality has nothing to do with a target-side loss-geometry bias. If input PCA alone fixed it, the whole account would need revisiting.

**Outcome (2026-08-03, 36 cells, 6 mice) — ⚠ MOSTLY INVALIDATED by a design error of mine, with one real result surviving and one prior cleanly falsified.**

**The invalidation first.** The run's baseline `weight_decay=1e-4` is **not scale-matched to flat weighting**, and it annihilates the network: in `A_flat_base` the input weights are **exactly zero** (‖W_in‖ = 0.0000 vs 3.29 for the evar baseline) and the decoder emits the uniform posterior (peakiness 0.0116 ≈ 1/91 = 0.0110, normalised loss 1.55). Mechanism: flat weighting puts 1/91 ≈ 0.011 on every PC where the evar weighting puts ~0.5 on the leading location PCs, so the fit-loss gradient is ~45× weaker while Adam's decay term `wd·θ` is unchanged — the decay wins. **30 of 36 cells are affected**, including the entire neural-PC ladder (`C_npc2`…`C_npc64` are identical to `A_flat_base` to 4 d.p.) and every OAT sweep cell. `B_flat_linear` is suppressed rather than annihilated (‖W_in‖ = 0.42 against **23.0** for its own wd=0 twin). This is the *mirror* of the documented `shape_lambda` trap, which inflates the loss and **dilutes** wd; flat_evar deflates the gradient and **amplifies** it.

**P1 — ↔ confirmed for spatial at wd=0, wrong on the loss value; the predicted cell itself is void.** The only clean flat cells are `*_wd0`. `A_flat_wd0` **spatial: peakiness 0.0603 vs target 0.0594 = 1.02×, normalised loss 0.948** (2/6 mice below chance) — against the evar baseline's 6.14× and 2.388. So flat weighting genuinely does fix spatial over-sharpening and does cross below chance, but the loss lands at 0.948, worse than the predicted 0.72–0.85 (shape30's 0.78) — the λ→∞ limit is *not* better than λ=0.3, it is slightly worse.

**P2 — ✗ FALSIFIED, and this is the substantive result.** Flat weighting + zero hidden units does **not** land on target. `B_flat_lin_wd0` **temporal: 0.3994 = 7.44× target**, and `A_flat_wd0` temporal 6.41× — versus the predicted ≤1.3×. Worse, the temporal **normalised loss goes UP**, from 8.18 (evar baseline) to **18.23**: flat weighting halves the temporal over-sharpening while making the decoder twice as bad. Spatial-linear reaches only 1.35× with normalised loss 1.438, i.e. above chance. **So removing the evar weighting fixes the spatial architecture and not the temporal one** — the loss-geometry account, which predicted a fix for both, is incomplete for the Jensen-averaged temporal decoder.
**Caveat I cannot yet rule out:** the entropy penalty `λ_H = 3e-3` is temporal-only and is *also* unscaled against a 45×-weaker fit gradient, so it is relatively far stronger under flat weighting — and it demonstrably sharpens (`A_flat_lam1em2` temporal hits 14.58×). The disentangling cell **flat + wd=0 + λ_H=0 does not exist in this run**, because the OAT sweep varies one axis at a time from a baseline that was itself broken. That cell is the top priority of `flatevar_v2`.

**P3 — ⚠ unanswerable.** Every sweep cell at the default wd is annihilated, so "do the other knobs matter under flat weighting" cannot be read from this run. The tell that something was wrong: peakiness identical to 4 d.p. across widths 2/4/8/16/32 and dropout 0/0.25/0.5 — knobs do not agree to four decimals, dead decoders do.

**P4 — ✗ ladder unanswerable, ✓ bonus prior confirmed.** The k-ladder is annihilated throughout. But the control `C_evar_npc16` (input PCA with the evar weighting kept, so it survived) gives peakiness **0.2400 = 4.5× target**, normalised loss 1.996 — against 0.3248 / 2.388 for the full population. **Input-side PCA alone does not fix the over-sharpening**, exactly as predicted; it shaves it modestly and stays far above target.

**Lessons.** (1) *Changing a loss's weighting changes its gradient scale, and every knob measured in gradient units — weight decay, and any additive penalty like λ_H — silently changes strength with it.* The GOTCHAS entry for `shape_lambda` documented one direction of this and I did not check the other. (2) **Identical metrics across genuinely different architectures is a bug signature, not a finding** — worth an automatic check in any sweep reporter. (3) A one-at-a-time sweep is only as good as its baseline: when the baseline is broken, every arm of the sweep is void, and the disentangling cell you need is precisely the one an OAT design does not contain.

## 2026-07-12 — DeepSets synthetic validation and real V1 uncertainty gain (registered before implementation/run)
**Synthetic prior:** mean-coded Q will be recoverable by all three models; variance-coded Q with matched trial means will be recoverable by moments and DeepSets but not mean-only; order-coded Q with identical unordered state sets will remain at null for all three. Confidence ~85%; falsifier: failure of these qualitative rankings under held-out common metrics.
**Real-data prior:** DeepSets will not show a robust six-mouse gain over the moments model for within-condition trialwise Q variance/entropy, although both may weakly beat mean-only in some mice. Confidence ~65%, based on the existing near-null within-condition uncertainty result. A consistent DeepSets > moments effect across mice would be a substantive surprise.
**Synthetic outcome (10 independent datasets) — ✓ confirmed for calibrated losses, with a loss-specific caveat:** KL/JS recovered the full qualitative pattern: mean-coded uncertainty was recoverable by all models; in the exact-mean-matched variance regime moments (KL 0.065) and DeepSets (0.115) beat mean-only (0.228); in the order regime all three were identical at KL ~0.23 / width error ~80 deg², while the explicit order oracle scored 0.88-0.99 vs 0.20 chance. The projection-trained branch failed common KL/width recovery even when the code was accessible, reproducing its known width-blind objective rather than an architecture failure. Brier learned but was less sample-efficient at the shared 120-epoch budget. Real-data outcome remains pending the six-mouse run.
**Real-data outcome (6 mice x all four losses, matched within-condition null) — ✓ central prior confirmed, with a raw-KL surprise:** DeepSets showed no width or entropy advantage over moments (KL-trained width delta -1.4 +/- 14.9 deg², p=0.93; entropy delta -0.0019 +/- 0.0119 nats, p=0.88), and no model beat its within-condition target-shuffle null (KL real/null: mean 0.990, moments 0.993, DeepSets 1.004; all p>=0.19). The surprise is that DeepSets nevertheless beat moments on *raw* held-out KL in 6/6 mice (delta -0.052, exact Wilcoxon p=0.031), but the same advantage is present under null training and therefore reflects architecture/condition decoding rather than trial-specific uncertainty. The condition-mean oracle is ~20x better still (KL ~0.014 vs neural ~0.2-0.7). Central conclusion: no evidence that unordered V1 temporal variability carries trial-specific IO uncertainty beyond condition.

## 2026-06-17 — train–val gap with dropout: does dropout regularise PCA's *overfitting* (even though it doesn't fix the over-sharpening)?  ↔ magnitude
**Registered (pre-resolution; p=0 baseline seen, p>0 still training):** the `monitor_val` knob finally lets us see the PCA train–val gap at patience 0. Prediction: dropout (p>0) will **shrink the train–val gap** (val_total−train_total at the final epoch) vs p=0 — because dropout *does* regularise capacity/overfitting — **even though** we already showed (2026-06-15) it does **not** reduce PCA's peakiness/over-sharpening. If both hold, the two are dissociated: PCA over-sharpens via loss-geometry drift, which is **not** the same thing as overfitting (a capacity/generalisation gap dropout can close). Confidence ~70%. Falsifier: if dropout leaves the gap unchanged, PCA's fit isn't capacity-limited here; if dropout shrinks the gap *and* fixes peakiness, the "over-sharpening ≠ overfitting" dissociation is wrong.
**Outcome (6 mice, PCA, patience 0 + monitor_val, all 3 levels) — ↔ over-predicted the effect:** the gap shrinks only **weakly and only for spatial** — spatial 0.0050→0.0048→**0.0044** (~12% at p=0.5, and via val *improving* 0.0062→0.0057, train flat ~0.0012); **temporal flat** 0.0055→0.0051→0.0053 (no trend). Far smaller than "dropout regularises overfitting" implies, and absent for temporal. (Baseline gap is real: val is 3–5× train spatial, ~2× temporal.)
**The richer finding (why the framing was slightly off):** the val PCA-loss curve is **flat, not rising** — it plateaus ~epoch 20 and stays there. So PCA is **not** overfitting in the textbook sense (train memorised, val degrading); the train–val gap is a **static offset baked into the location-only loss from early on**, not progressive overfitting that dropout could undo. And this curve is **not where the over-sharpening lives**: peakiness is invisible to the PCA loss (loss-blind subspace), so early-stop fixes peakiness *despite* a flat val-loss (the over-sharpening drifts up monotonically with epochs even as val-PCA-loss is flat), while dropout barely moves either.
**Lesson:** "train–val gap" and "over-sharpening" are **different objects** — the gap is a static loss-geometry offset (dropout-insensitive, doesn't grow with epochs), the over-sharpening is a monotonic drift in the loss-blind subspace (early-stop-sensitive, smoothness-fixable). Don't conflate the generalisation gap with the calibration pathology. Still consistent with the loss-geometry account (capacity knobs don't fix the calibration problem); the prediction's *spirit* (dropout ≠ the fix) held, its *mechanism* (a closable overfitting gap) didn't.

## 2026-06-16 — output-smoothness penalty: does the loss-side fix tame PCA?  ✓ confirmed
**Registered (pre-run):** unlike the capacity knobs, a smoothness penalty (Σ(Δp)² on the decoded posterior) *should* work — it acts directly on the high-freq spikes; expect λ_smooth↑ to pull PCA peakiness→target and drop KL, with a U-shaped KL vs λ_smooth (optimum at moderate λ, over-smoothing beyond). Confidence ~80%.
**Outcome (local, PCA, λ_smooth=0.1 vs noreg):** confirmed — peakiness 0.347→0.087 (spat) / 0.715→0.133 (temp), KL 1.30→0.65 / 4.61→1.04 (IO target peakiness 0.059). At λ=0.1 it already calibrates PCA *better than early stopping* (temporal KL 1.04 vs early-stop's 1.75). The U-shape / over-smoothing onset still needs the λ_smooth sweep (one point so far).
**Lesson:** the loss-geometry account *predicts the fix* — the pathology lives in the loss-blind high-freq subspace, so only a term that constrains THAT subspace (smoothness, or shape_lambda's Brier) fixes it; capacity knobs can't. The model is now 4-for-4: λ_H ✗ (sharpens), dropout ✓ (no help), averaging ✓ (no help), smoothness ✓ (works).
**Sweep resolved (2026-06-16):** direction ✓ but **no U-shape in [0,1]** (↔ on the shape) — PCA KL falls monotonically to λ=1 (temporal 1.75→0.48), peakiness lands on the IO target at λ≈0.3; KL min at the λ=1 boundary, so the over-smoothing→KL-rise onset is beyond λ=1 (I over-predicted the onset). Bonus: **Wasserstein doesn't respond** at λ≤1 (KL ~flat) — NOT for lack of spikes (the gallery shows it's spiky) but a **loss-scale mismatch**: its fit-loss is ~2800× PCA's (Wass ≈13.6 vs PCA ≈0.005), so the same λ_smooth is negligible against it. (Implication: a loss-general smoothness term should scale λ_smooth by the fit-loss magnitude. I initially mis-attributed this to "Wass isn't spiky" — corrected via the gallery + the fit-loss ratio.)

## 2026-06-16 — weight/output averaging (SWA): does late-epoch averaging tame PCA?  ✓ confirmed
**Registered (pre-run):** if PCA's over-sharpening is a *monotonic* drift, SWA (avg late weights) lands near the late sharp state → little help; output-avg could still smooth bin-to-bin spikes *if* they wander across epochs. Confidence ~70%.
**Outcome (noreg, 200 ep, 21 snapshots, 6 mice):** both barely move PCA — peakiness 0.347→0.347 (spat) / 0.715→0.716 (temp), KL 1.30→1.29 / 4.62→4.50. Gallery: final/SWA/output-avg PCA posteriors are exactly superimposed — the spikes sit at the *same* bins every late epoch. So the drift is monotonic AND the spikes are stable (not wandering), and averaging reproduces them. Minor generic benefit for the milder losses (CE/KL/Wass spatial KL ~6–14% lower).
**Lesson:** averaging fights *oscillatory* over-fitting noise; PCA's over-sharpening is a stable directional drift in the loss-blind subspace, so it's immune — same loss-geometry model as the λ_H ✗ / dropout ✓. The only levers are the loss (width/smoothness term) or stopping early.

## 2026-06-15 — dropout vs early stopping: does dropout rescue PCA's calibration?  ✓ confirmed
**Registered (pre-run, in chat before the cluster launch):** dropout will NOT specifically rescue PCA's calibration/peakiness — that's a loss-geometry problem (width-blind subspace), not capacity/overfitting; expect PCA over-confident at every dropout level, while early stopping helps. Confidence ~70%.
**Outcome (6 mice; peakiness + raw KL, regime-independent):** confirmed. Early-stop ~halves PCA peakiness (temporal 0.72→0.36) and raw KL (4.6→1.75); dropout leaves PCA peakiness unchanged (~0.72 at p=0.1/0.25/0.5) and slightly *worsens* spatial-PCA KL (1.30→1.67). CE/KL/JS already calibrated. (Train–val gap unmeasured — val⟺early-stop coupling; needs `monitor_val`.)
**Note:** right direction after the λ_H ✗ — the *same* model (PCA's over-sharpening is loss-geometry, not capacity) predicts both: capacity/penalty knobs (dropout, λ_H) don't fix it; only changing the loss (width term) or catching the drift early (early-stop) does.

## 2026-06-14 — λ_H sweep: effect on the temporal decoder  ✗ direction (+ ⚠ invalidated assumption)
**Registered (pre-launch, 2026-06-14, in-session before the gpu1 run):** rising λ_H broadens temporal posteriors and pulls temporal-PCA KL-skill down toward 1, with an optimum λ_H≈0.03–0.1; CE/KL flat; spatial λ_H-invariant. Confidence ~60%.
**Outcome (6 mice, λ_H∈{0, 1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2, 3e-2, 0.1}):** temporal-PCA KL-skill rose **monotonically 2.29→3.41 (worse)** and peakiness **0.35→0.49 (peakier)**; Wasserstein 1.37→1.72; JS over-confident only at λ_H=0.1; CE/KL immune (~0.5, flat); spatial flat (✓ that sub-prediction held). Validated real, not a bug: spatial control flat, and `entlam0p003` ≈ `loss_comparison_v1` (2.20 vs 2.17).
**Why wrong:** the entropy term is `total = fit_loss + λ·H(pred)`, minimised → drives H *down* → it **sharpens** (penalises entropy), opposite to the max-entropy / broadening regulariser I assumed. The "spatial flat" part was correct.
**Lesson:** check the **sign** of a penalty/loss term — or the relevant `GOTCHAS.md` entry — before registering a prior about its direction. GOTCHAS already said "the SBC entropy penalty enforces sample-sharpness; the data don't want this commitment."
**Correction (same day, after Theo flagged it):** the prior itself *misframed the experiment* — penalising entropy obviously sharpens; that was never the question. The real question: can **peaky per-bin (instantaneous) samples coexist with a broad, calibrated time-average** under the information-theoretic losses? Re-analysed with `diagnostics/lambda_h_perbin_vs_avg.py` (per-bin vs time-avg entropy): **CE/KL inert** (per-bin H ≈3.65 ≈ IO target across all λ_H; sampling spread flat ~0.26 → bins are broad copies of the average); **JS is the one info-theoretic loss that tolerates λ_H** (per-bin 3.71→3.28, spread 0.23→0.37, average still ≈ target at λ_H=0.1); PCA/Wasserstein get peaky bins but an uncalibrated average. **Deeper lesson (the real one):** understand what an experiment is *testing* before forming a prior on its outcome — I predicted the time-averaged direction, which was not the question being asked.
